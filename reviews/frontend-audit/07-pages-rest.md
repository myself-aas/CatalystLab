## Cascade facts used below (from reading CSS, not from a browser run)

These are not in the brief's token table, but most dark-mode findings depend on them. Please confirm them in a browser once.

- In dark mode, `.ds-card` stays **white**. `patch.css:122` sets `--p-card: #FFFFFF` ("bento white card on dark canvas"), and `patch.css:698` applies it. The card's own text colour is `var(--text-primary)` (`index.css:1234`), which is **#F7FDFF** in dark (`index.css:383`). Unclassed text inside a `ds-card` is therefore near-white on white.
- `patch.css:1508-1556` corrects only these classes inside a dark `ds-card`: `text-foreground`, `text-muted-foreground`, `text-foreground-muted`, `text-cream*`, and the hue `text-*` classes. `text-primary` (#F7FDFF) and `text-info` (#22D3EE) are **not** corrected. `<pre>` is excluded from all of these rules.
- The descendant rule forces `text-foreground` to #1C1E21 **even when a descendant also has a dark background**. `bg-background` becomes #111827 (`patch.css:1183`), `bg-surface*` becomes #2C2F32 (`:1184`), and `bg-card` becomes #2C3032. The same happens when the card itself has `bg-surface`/`bg-card`, which beat `--p-card` through `!important`. Result: #1C1E21 text on a #111827 or #2C2F32 surface, about 1.1 to 1.3:1.
- The input colour rules in `patch.css:848-860` have specificity (0,1,1). They lose to `html.dark .ds-card [class*="text-foreground"]…`, which is (0,3,3), and to `html.dark [class*="bg-background"]`, which is (0,2,1). As a result, typed input text follows the two broken rules above.
- `html.dark p` (`patch.css:834`) is unlayered. It sets every `<p>` with no stronger rule to rgba(240,250,255,.65). This includes `.framer-body-text`, which is only (0,1,0) at `patch.css:831`.
